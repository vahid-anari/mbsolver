The molecule has three states $|1\rangle$, $|2\rangle$, $|3\rangle$, ordered
by energy, $E_1 < E_2 < E_3$, with free Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
  \hat{H}_0 = \sum_{i} E_i\,|i\rangle\langle i| .
\end{equation}
For each pair $i > j$ we write the transition frequency and wave number as
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{ij} = \frac{E_i - E_j}{\hbar}, \qquad k_{ij} = \frac{\omega_{ij}}{c},
\end{equation}
so that $\omega_{31} = \omega_{32} + \omega_{21}$. The transition dipoles
are $\bm{d}_{ij} = \langle i|\hat{\bm{d}}|j\rangle$ with
$\bm{d}_{ji} = \bm{d}_{ij}^*$. The equations below are derived with all three
dipoles nonzero; setting one of them to zero gives the configurations of
Fig.~\ref{fig:levels}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
  \toprule
  Configuration & Shared level & Allowed transitions & Forbidden \\
  \midrule
  $\Lambda$ & upper, $|3\rangle$  & $3\rightarrow1$, $3\rightarrow2$ & $d_{21} = 0$ \\
  V         & lower, $|1\rangle$  & $2\rightarrow1$, $3\rightarrow1$ & $d_{32} = 0$ \\
  ladder    & middle, $|2\rangle$ & $2\rightarrow1$, $3\rightarrow2$ & $d_{31} = 0$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The case with all three dipoles nonzero, a closed loop, is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:loop}; the solver does not treat it.

\section{Fields and Density Matrix}

\subsection{Fields}

The electric field is the sum of three components, one resonant with each
transition,
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\E}(z,t) = \sum_{i>j} \hat{\bm{e}}_{ij}\,\E_{ij}(z,t),
  \qquad
  \E_{ij}(z,t) = \tfrac{1}{2}E_{ij}(z,t)\,e^{-i\varphi_{ij}} + \mathrm{c.c.},
  \qquad
  \varphi_{ij} = \omega_{ij}t - k_{ij}z,
  \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
where $E_{ij}$ are slowly varying complex envelopes and
$\hat{\bm{e}}_{ij}$ fixed polarization vectors. Each component is a plane wave
propagating along $z$; transverse effects such as diffraction are neglected.
The full Hamiltonian is $\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 - \hat{\bm{d}}\cdot\bm{\E}$, and
the $i\rightarrow j$ transition couples to its own field through the dipole
component
\begin{equation}
  d_{ij} = \hat{\bm{e}}_{ij}\cdot\bm{d}_{ij}, \qquad d_{ji} = d_{ij}^* .
\end{equation}
Diagonal elements of $\hat{\bm{d}}$ give only non-resonant terms and are
dropped in the rotating-wave approximation below.

\subsection{Density matrix}

The molecules at position $z$ are described by the single-molecule density
matrix $\hat{\rho}(z,t) = \sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}\,|i\rangle\langle j|$ with
$\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho} = 1$. Its diagonal elements $n_i = \rho_{ii}$ are
the fractional populations, and the population differences are
\begin{equation}
  n_{ij} = n_i - n_j \qquad (i > j).
\end{equation}
A positive $n_{ij}$ means inversion on the $i\rightarrow j$ transition. The
coherences carry the carrier of their transition,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}R_{ij}(z,t)\,e^{-i\varphi_{ij}},
  \qquad \rho_{ji} = \rho_{ij}^* \qquad (i > j),
  \label{eq:coh}
\end{equation}
with slowly varying amplitudes $R_{ij}$.

\subsection{Approximations}

All envelopes vary slowly compared with their carriers (SVEA),
\begin{equation}
  \left|\pderiv{E_{ij}}{t}\right| \ll \omega_{ij}|E_{ij}|, \qquad
  \left|\pderiv{E_{ij}}{z}\right| \ll k_{ij}|E_{ij}|,
\end{equation}
and likewise for $R_{ij}$ \cite{GrossHaroche1982,Benedict1996}. In the
rotating-wave approximation (RWA), each density-matrix element keeps only the
terms that oscillate at its own carrier. This also assumes that each field
drives only its own transition: the transition frequencies must differ by much
more than the Rabi frequencies and the dephasing rates. All fields propagate
along $+z$ with phase velocity $c$, so that $k_{31} = k_{32} + k_{21}$ and the
three carriers keep the same relative phase throughout the sample. Detunings
are set to zero.

\section{Equations of Motion}

\subsection{Bloch equations}

Substituting Eqs.~(\ref{eq:field})--(\ref{eq:coh}) into the Liouville--von
Neumann equation $i\hbar\,\partial_t\hat{\rho} = [\hat{H},\hat{\rho}]$ and
keeping the resonant terms gives the Bloch equations. With
\begin{equation}
  W_{ij} = -\frac{1}{2\hbar}\operatorname{Im}\!\left(d_{ij}^* E_{ij}^* R_{ij}\right)
  \qquad (i > j),
  \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
the populations obey
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{n_1}{t} = W_{21} + W_{31}, \qquad
  \pderiv{n_2}{t} = W_{32} - W_{21}, \qquad
  \pderiv{n_3}{t} = -W_{31} - W_{32},
  \label{eq:pop}
\end{equation}
which conserve $n_1 + n_2 + n_3$, and the coherences obey
\begin{align}
  \pderiv{R_{21}}{t} &= -\frac{i}{\hbar}d_{21}E_{21}\,n_{21}
     + \frac{i}{2\hbar}\left(d_{32}^*E_{32}^*R_{31} - d_{31}E_{31}R_{32}^*\right),\\
  \pderiv{R_{31}}{t} &= -\frac{i}{\hbar}d_{31}E_{31}\,n_{31}
     - \frac{i}{2\hbar}\left(d_{21}E_{21}R_{32} - d_{32}E_{32}R_{21}\right),\\
  \pderiv{R_{32}}{t} &= -\frac{i}{\hbar}d_{32}E_{32}\,n_{32}
     - \frac{i}{2\hbar}\left(d_{21}^*E_{21}^*R_{31} - d_{31}E_{31}R_{21}^*\right).
\end{align}
The second terms couple the transitions: each coherence is driven by the
product of the field on one transition and the coherence on another.

\subsection{Field equations}

In a medium of $n_0$ molecules per unit volume, the macroscopic polarization
$\bm{\mathcal{P}} = n_0\operatorname{Tr}(\hat{\rho}\,\hat{\bm{d}})$ has, along
$\hat{\bm{e}}_{ij}$ and at the carrier of the $i\rightarrow j$ transition, the
component
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P}_{ij} = \frac{n_0}{2}\,d_{ij}^*R_{ij}\,e^{-i\varphi_{ij}} + \mathrm{c.c.}
\end{equation}
Substituting the field and this polarization into the one-dimensional wave
equation
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{^2\E_{ij}}{z^2} - \frac{1}{c^2}\pderiv{^2\E_{ij}}{t^2}
  = \frac{1}{\epsilon_0 c^2}\pderiv{^2\mathcal{P}_{ij}}{t^2}
\end{equation}
and dropping second derivatives of the envelopes (SVEA) gives
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{E_{ij}}{z} + \frac{1}{c}\pderiv{E_{ij}}{t}
  = i\,\frac{n_0 k_{ij} d_{ij}^*}{2\epsilon_0}\,R_{ij}.
  \label{eq:max}
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~(\ref{eq:W}) this gives
$\partial_z|E_{ij}|^2 = (2\hbar n_0 k_{ij}/\epsilon_0)\,W_{ij}$ in the
co-moving frame: $W_{ij}$ is the net rate at which the field on the
$i\rightarrow j$ transition moves population from $i$ to $j$, and it is
positive when that field is amplified.

\section{Retarded Time and Dimensionless Form}
\label{sec:dimless}

\subsection{Retarded time}

In the retarded time $\tret = t - z/c$, which serves all three fields, the
operator $\partial_z + c^{-1}\partial_t$ becomes $\partial_z$ at fixed
$\tret$, and the field equations become pure propagation equations, while
the Bloch equations keep their form with $t \rightarrow \tret$.

\subsection{Scaled variables}

Lengths are scaled by the sample length $L$ and times by a time unit $\T$,
\begin{equation}
  \xi = \frac{z}{L}, \qquad \tau = \frac{\tret}{\T},
\end{equation}
and the fields by their Rabi frequencies,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\tE_{ij} = -i\,\frac{\T d_{ij}}{\hbar}\,E_{ij}.}
  \label{eq:Etilde}
\end{equation}
All dipole phases are absorbed into $\tE_{ij}$. The scaled transfer rates are
\begin{equation}
  \tW_{ij} = \T W_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\tE_{ij}^* R_{ij}\right).
\end{equation}
The field equations become
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{\tE_{ij}}{\xi} = \eta_{ij}\,R_{ij},
  \qquad
  \boxed{\eta_{ij} = \frac{\T}{\TR{ij}}, \qquad
  \TR{ij} = \frac{2\epsilon_0\hbar}{n_0 L k_{ij} |d_{ij}|^2},}
  \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
where $\TR{ij}$ is the cooperation time of the $i\rightarrow j$ transition.
The time unit $\T$ is free: only the couplings $\eta_{ij}$ enter the
equations, and their ratios,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\eta_{ij}}{\eta_{kl}} = \frac{|d_{ij}|^2 k_{ij}}{|d_{kl}|^2 k_{kl}},
\end{equation}
are fixed by the two transitions. A common choice is $\T = \TR{ij}$ for one
transition, which makes its coupling one.

\begin{notebox}
  \textbf{Sign of the field equation.} Here $\partial\tE/\partial\xi = +\eta R$,
  while the two-level model has $\partial A'/\partial z' = -\eta R$. Both use
  the same carrier $e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$ and the same coherence envelope
  (upper--lower element $= \tfrac12 R\,e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$); they differ only
  in the phase of the field scale. The two-level model uses
  $A' = A/A_0$ with $A_0 = -i\hbar/(d\,\T)$, i.e.\ $A' = +i\,d\,\T A/\hbar$,
  while Eq.~(\ref{eq:Etilde}) has $-i$. For the same physical field
  $A' = -\tE$, which flips the sign of every field term: the two-level
  equations $\partial_{\tau'} w = A'R$, $\partial_{\tau'} R = -A'w$,
  $\partial_{z'}A' = -\eta R$ correspond here to
  $\partial_\tau n_{ij} = -\tE_{ij}R_{ij}$,
  $\partial_\tau R_{ij} = \tE_{ij}n_{ij}$, $\partial_\xi\tE_{ij} = \eta_{ij}R_{ij}$
  for a single transition. Intensities $|\tE|^2$ are the same in both
  conventions.
\end{notebox}

\subsection{Dimensionless equations}

With Eq.~(\ref{eq:Etilde}), the Bloch and field equations become

\begin{resultbox}
\begin{align}
  \pderiv{n_1}{\tau} &= \tW_{21} + \tW_{31}, \qquad
  \pderiv{n_2}{\tau} = \tW_{32} - \tW_{21}, \qquad
  \pderiv{n_3}{\tau} = -\tW_{31} - \tW_{32},
  \label{eq:dimpop}\\[6pt]
  \pderiv{R_{21}}{\tau} &= \tE_{21}\,n_{21}
     + \tfrac12\left(\tE_{31}R_{32}^* + \tE_{32}^*R_{31}\right),
  \label{eq:dimR21}\\[4pt]
  \pderiv{R_{31}}{\tau} &= \tE_{31}\,n_{31}
     + \tfrac12\left(\tE_{21}R_{32} - \tE_{32}R_{21}\right),
  \label{eq:dimR31}\\[4pt]
  \pderiv{R_{32}}{\tau} &= \tE_{32}\,n_{32}
     - \tfrac12\left(\tE_{21}^*R_{31} + \tE_{31}R_{21}^*\right),
  \label{eq:dimR32}\\[6pt]
  \pderiv{\tE_{ij}}{\xi} &= \eta_{ij}\,R_{ij}.
  \label{eq:dimE}
\end{align}
\end{resultbox}

\begin{notebox}
  No dipole appears in a denominator. A zero dipole $d_{ij}$ gives
  $\eta_{ij} = 0$ for any $\T$ and, with no incident field on that transition,
  $\tE_{ij} = 0$ everywhere. Setting one dipole to zero in
  Eqs.~(\ref{eq:dimpop})--(\ref{eq:dimE}) is therefore exact.
\end{notebox}

\section{The Three Configurations}
\label{sec:configs}

With the field of the forbidden transition set to zero, the populations enter
only through the differences $n_a$ and $n_b$ of the two allowed transitions.
From Eq.~(\ref{eq:dimpop}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
  \toprule
  Configuration & Zero field & $n_a$, $n_b$ & $\partial_\tau n_a$ (coherent) & $\partial_\tau n_b$ (coherent) \\
  \midrule
  $\Lambda$ & $\tE_{21}$ & $n_{31}$, $n_{32}$ & $-(2\tW_{31} + \tW_{32})$ & $-(\tW_{31} + 2\tW_{32})$ \\
  V         & $\tE_{32}$ & $n_{21}$, $n_{31}$ & $-(2\tW_{21} + \tW_{31})$ & $-(\tW_{21} + 2\tW_{31})$ \\
  ladder    & $\tE_{31}$ & $n_{21}$, $n_{32}$ & $-(2\tW_{21} - \tW_{32})$ & $-(2\tW_{32} - \tW_{21})$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In the $\Lambda$ configuration, emission on either transition empties the
shared upper level and lowers both inversions: the two transitions compete
for one population. The V configuration has the same structure with the
shared level at the bottom: emission on either transition fills level~1 and
again lowers both inversions.

The ladder differs in the sign of the cross term. Emission on the upper step,
$\tW_{32} > 0$, fills level~2 and therefore \emph{raises} $n_{21}$, while
emission on the lower step, $\tW_{21} > 0$, empties level~2 and raises
$n_{32}$. The two steps feed each other's inversion in a cascade.

In each configuration the coherence of the forbidden pair, $R_{21}$ for
$\Lambda$, $R_{32}$ for V and $R_{31}$ for the ladder, carries no field and is
generated only through the product terms of
Eqs.~(\ref{eq:dimR21})--(\ref{eq:dimR32}). If one allowed transition starts
with neither coherence nor incident field, it stays without both, the
forbidden coherence stays zero, and the model reduces to the two-level
equations for the other transition. A nonzero initial coherence or incident
field on both allowed transitions is needed for the three-level coupling to
act.

\section{Relaxation and Pumping}
\label{sec:relax}

Non-coherent processes are added phenomenologically. The two population
differences of the allowed transitions relax towards their reference values
with times $T_a^{(1)}$, $T_b^{(1)}$, and pumps $\Lambda_a$, $\Lambda_b$ act on
them:
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{n_a}{\tau} = \left(\pderiv{n_a}{\tau}\right)_{\!\text{coh}}
     - \frac{n_a - n_a^0}{\tau_a^{(1)}} + \tilde{\Lambda}_a ,
  \label{eq:relaxn}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $n_b$. All three coherences dephase towards their reference
values,
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{R_{ij}}{\tau} = \left(\pderiv{R_{ij}}{\tau}\right)_{\!\text{coh}}
     - \frac{R_{ij} - R_{ij}^0}{\tau_{ij}^{(2)}} ,
  \label{eq:relaxR}
\end{equation}
with the scaled times and pumps
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{ij}^{(k)} = \frac{T_{ij}^{(k)}}{\T}, \qquad
  \tilde{\Lambda}_{ij} = \T\,\Lambda_{ij}.
\end{equation}
These terms represent the net effect of pumping and non-coherent transfer
through the full level structure of the molecule; they need not correspond to
direct transfer rates among the three levels. The equations contain the
populations only through $n_a$ and $n_b$, so these two differences, the
coherences and the fields form a closed system. The total population
$n_1 + n_2 + n_3$, which pumping and relaxation need not conserve, does not
enter.

\section{Real-Variable Reduction}
\label{subsec:real}

If the initial coherences $R_{ij}^0$ and the entrance fields
$\tE_{ij}(0,\tau)$ are real, the right-hand sides of
Eqs.~(\ref{eq:dimpop})--(\ref{eq:dimE}) and
(\ref{eq:relaxn})--(\ref{eq:relaxR}) are real whenever the variables are, so
all variables stay real. Then $\tW_{ij} = \tfrac12\tE_{ij}R_{ij}$ and the
system becomes

\begin{resultbox}
\begin{align}
  \pderiv{n_a}{\tau} &= \left(\pderiv{n_a}{\tau}\right)_{\!\text{coh}}
     - \frac{n_a - n_a^0}{\tau_a^{(1)}} + \tilde{\Lambda}_a,
  \qquad \text{and likewise for } n_b,
  \label{eq:rn}\\[6pt]
  \pderiv{R_{21}}{\tau} &= \tE_{21}\,n_{21}
     + \tfrac12\left(\tE_{31}R_{32} + \tE_{32}R_{31}\right)
     - \frac{R_{21} - R_{21}^0}{\tau_{21}^{(2)}},\\[4pt]
  \pderiv{R_{31}}{\tau} &= \tE_{31}\,n_{31}
     + \tfrac12\left(\tE_{21}R_{32} - \tE_{32}R_{21}\right)
     - \frac{R_{31} - R_{31}^0}{\tau_{31}^{(2)}},\\[4pt]
  \pderiv{R_{32}}{\tau} &= \tE_{32}\,n_{32}
     - \tfrac12\left(\tE_{21}R_{31} + \tE_{31}R_{21}\right)
     - \frac{R_{32} - R_{32}^0}{\tau_{32}^{(2)}},\\[6pt]
  \pderiv{\tE_{ij}}{\xi} &= \eta_{ij}\,R_{ij},
  \label{eq:rE}
\end{align}
\end{resultbox}

with the coherent parts of $\partial_\tau n_a$ and $\partial_\tau n_b$ from
the table of Section~\ref{sec:configs}, now with
$\tW_{ij} = \tfrac12\tE_{ij}R_{ij}$, and the field of the forbidden transition
zero.

\begin{notebox}
  Equations~(\ref{eq:rn})--(\ref{eq:rE}) are the system solved by
  \code{mbsolver.three\_level.solve\_1d}: fourth-order Runge--Kutta in
  $\tau$ at each $\xi$, and an explicit step in $\xi$ for the fields. They
  hold unchanged when the relaxation times depend on time.
\end{notebox}

\section{Initial and Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:ics}

\subsection{Populations}

The initial populations $n_1^0$, $n_2^0$, $n_3^0$ are given directly, with
$n_1^0 + n_2^0 + n_3^0 = 1$ and $n_i^0 \ge 0$. They fix the initial
differences $n_{ij}^0 = n_i^0 - n_j^0$, which are also the reference values
of the relaxation terms, the same at every $\xi$.

\subsection{Coherences}

The magnitude of each initial coherence is bounded by the populations
(Cauchy--Schwarz),
\begin{equation}
  |R_{ij}^0| \le \mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\max} = 2\sqrt{n_i^0 n_j^0},
\end{equation}
and is given as a fraction $c_{ij}$ of this bound,
\begin{equation}
  R_{ij}^0 = c_{ij}\,\mathcal{R}_{ij}^{\max}, \qquad |c_{ij}| \le 1.
\end{equation}
A small $c_{ij}$ on an inverted transition seeds its emission, as $\theta_0$
does in the two-level model.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

The fields entering the sample, $\tE_{ij}(0,\tau)$, are given for the two
allowed transitions; for emission generated by the medium itself they are
zero. With uniform initial coherences, Eq.~(\ref{eq:rE}) gives the fields at
the exit at $\tau = 0$:
$\tE_{ij}(1,0) = \tE_{ij}(0,0) + \eta_{ij} R_{ij}^0$.

\section{Physical Scales}
\label{sec:scales}

\subsection{Cooperation times}

With the Einstein coefficient of the $i\rightarrow j$ transition,
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{ij} = \frac{\omega_{ij}^3 |d_{ij}|^2}{3\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c^3}
  = \frac{k_{ij}^3 |d_{ij}|^2}{3\pi\epsilon_0\hbar},
\end{equation}
the cooperation time~(\ref{eq:eta}) takes the two-level form
\begin{equation}
  \TR{ij} = \frac{2\epsilon_0\hbar}{n_0 L k_{ij} |d_{ij}|^2}
     = \frac{2 k_{ij}^2}{3\pi\,\Gamma_{ij}\, n_0 L}
     = \frac{8\pi}{3\,\Gamma_{ij}\, n_0 L \lambda_{ij}^2}.
\end{equation}
Both forms agree when $\Gamma_{ij}$ is computed from $d_{ij}$; with a
measured decay rate they differ by the ratio of the two rates.

\subsection{Fields, intensities and pumps}

The dimensionless fields are Rabi frequencies, Eq.~(\ref{eq:Etilde}). Each
transition has its own field and intensity scale,
\begin{equation}
  E_0^{(ij)} = \frac{\hbar}{\T |d_{ij}|}, \qquad
  I_0^{(ij)} = \tfrac12\epsilon_0 c \bigl(E_0^{(ij)}\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
so that the field amplitude is $|E_{ij}| = E_0^{(ij)}|\tE_{ij}|$ and the
cycle-averaged intensity is
\begin{equation}
  I_{ij} = I_0^{(ij)}\,|\tE_{ij}|^2 .
\end{equation}
A pump rate $\Lambda$ in $\mathrm{m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$ (molecules per volume and
time) corresponds to the dimensionless pump $\tilde{\Lambda} = \Lambda/\Lambda_0$
with $\Lambda_0 = n_0/\T$.

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Closed Loop}
\label{sec:loop}

When all three dipoles are nonzero, Eqs.~(\ref{eq:dimpop})--(\ref{eq:dimE})
still hold, but the dynamics depends on the loop phase
$\arg(\tE_{31}\tE_{32}^*\tE_{21}^*)$, which includes the phase of
$d_{31}^*d_{32}d_{21}$ and cannot be removed by rephasing the coherences
\cite{Kosachiov1992}; the real restriction fixes it at $0$ or $\pi$. The
three population differences are then not independent, and the relaxation of
Section~\ref{sec:relax} must be replaced by a model for the populations
themselves. A closed loop requires levels without definite parity, as in
symmetry-broken artificial atoms \cite{Liu2005}; it does not occur for
electric-dipole transitions between parity eigenstates. The solver does not
treat this case.

Three-level Bloch equations in coherence-vector form were given by Hioe and
Eberly \cite{HioeEberly1981}, and the simultaneous propagation of two fields
in three-level media was treated by Konopnicki and Eberly
\cite{KonopnickiEberly1981}.

\section{In the Code}

\subsection{Solver}

\code{mbsolver.three\_level.solve\_1d} takes the configuration and the
dimensionless parameters (times in units of $\T$) and returns the variables
on \code{n\_planes} evenly spaced planes $\xi \in [0, 1]$. Parameters are
given only for the allowed transitions of the chosen configuration
($ij = 31, 32$ for $\Lambda$; $21, 31$ for V; $21, 32$ for the ladder);
\code{t2\_ij} exists for all three coherences.

\subsection{Physical scales}

\code{mbsolver.three\_level.scaling} takes $\T$ (s), $L$ (m), $n_0$
(m$^{-3}$) and, for each allowed transition, its frequency
$\nu_{ij} = \omega_{ij}/2\pi$ (Hz) and either its dipole moment $|d_{ij}|$
(C\,m; \code{mbsolver.constants.DEBYE} converts from debye) or its
Einstein coefficient $\Gamma_{ij}$ (s$^{-1}$). It returns, for each
transition, $\eta_{ij}$, $\TR{ij}$, $\Gamma_{ij}$, $|d_{ij}|$, $k_{ij}$,
$\lambda_{ij}$, $E_0^{(ij)}$ and $I_0^{(ij)}$, and $\Lambda_0$
(Section~\ref{sec:scales}).
